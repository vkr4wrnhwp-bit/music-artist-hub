SB-11 SNP-1 Snippet Finder · Reels · Shorts · TikTok

Two passes: the loudest sustained 15-second window, then the loudest 30 — each then snapped to the nearest downbeat.

Scans your track's real energy for the loudest sustained 15- and 30-second stretches — level detection, not taste; trust your ears on the final pick. The window is then snapped to the nearest downbeat using the same beat grid SB-13 measures, because a clip that starts mid-beat reads as a mistake however good the audio is. It only snaps when the grid is confident and the move is under half a bar — a hook dragged most of a bar to reach a downbeat is no longer the hook it found — and the panel says which of those happened. Preview plays that window, Snippet WAV bounces it through the whole rack, and Vertical video records a 720×1280 WebM with a live spectrum ring (keep this tab visible while it records; phone editors convert WebM to MP4).

BPM comes from a spectral-flux onset envelope autocorrelated over 60–190 BPM; key from a 12-class chroma correlated against the Krumhansl-Schmuckler major and minor profiles. Both are estimates with a score attached, not readings off a tag — autocorrelation genuinely cannot tell a tempo from its half or double, so both are offered rather than guessed at. Stretching is WSOLA and pitch-shifting is resample-plus-stretch: honest time-domain DSP that holds up at the ratios people actually use, and smears transients if you push it far. Runs in this tab.

Six separate processes, not one effect with six names — and the glow is a meter, not decoration: each tube lights by how hard that stage is actually being driven right now, and V6 lights by its real gain reduction. A stage doing nothing stays dark, and at zero every stage is mathematically the identity, so off is genuinely off. V1 adds even harmonics via a squaring term (measured: 3× more 2nd harmonic than 3rd) with a DC blocker after it, because asymmetry makes DC. V2 is odd-symmetric, so it can only make odd harmonics. V3 is a soft-knee curve plus the two filter moves that make tape sound like tape. V4 is two LFOs on a delay line — real pitch movement. V5 saturates the low band only, where a transformer core actually saturates. V6 is slow program-dependent compression. They sit after the tube stage and before the cab, so the compressor downstream hears the colour.

These are the figures the platforms publish, not something this app checks with them, and they do move. Every service turns a loud master down — that costs nothing. Turning a quiet one up is where the true peak comes up with it, which is the only case that can clip.

Peak metering tells you about clipping and nothing about how loud you'll actually sound — this is the number every platform normalises by. Measured to the standard, not approximated: K-weighting derived for your file's sample rate rather than the 48 kHz coefficients printed in the spec, 400 ms blocks with both required gates (absolute at −70 LUFS, relative at −10 LU) so silence and quiet passages can't drag the average down, loudness range per EBU 3342, and a true peak found by 4× oversampling because the peak between samples is what converters and lossy encoders actually see. That method under-reads by up to about 0.7 dB in the worst case, which is why mastering ceilings sit at −1 dBTP rather than 0. Verified against the published EBU Tech 3341 compliance cases.
