SB-14
VLV-6
Valve Bank · six colour stages
AB
Explain

Six separate processes, not one effect with six names — and the glow is a meter, not decoration: each tube lights by how hard that stage is actually being driven right now, and V6 lights by its real gain reduction. A stage doing nothing stays dark, and at zero every stage is mathematically the identity, so off is genuinely off. V1 adds even harmonics via a squaring term (measured: 3× more 2nd harmonic than 3rd) with a DC blocker after it, because asymmetry makes DC. V2 is odd-symmetric, so it can only make odd harmonics. V3 is a soft-knee curve plus the two filter moves that make tape sound like tape. V4 is two LFOs on a delay line — real pitch movement. V5 saturates the low band only, where a transformer core actually saturates. V6 is slow program-dependent compression. They sit after the tube stage and before the cab, so the compressor downstream hears the colour.

    SB-01
    Harmonic Bank
    Tube Stage
    AB
    Explain

    Asymmetric waveshaping — the even-harmonic warmth tubes are loved for. An emulation, and labeled as one. Drive sets how hard the curve bends; Bias skews it so the two halves of the wave bend differently — that asymmetry is where the even harmonics come from; Mix blends the shaped signal against the clean one.

    Every unit's ON/OFF kills only that module (knobs hold their positions, export matches), and A/B swaps back to the settings you had before your last tweaks.

    SB-02
    Foundation
    SUB-1 · Psychoacoustic Lows
    AB
    Explain

    Not a bass boost: Depth is a deep 45Hz shelf, Shake a 42Hz room resonance, Growl synthesizes sub harmonics so small speakers hear lows they can't play — psychoacoustics, not magic. A 24 Hz high-pass guards the bottom; the growl path takes only the sub band (under 120 Hz), bends it through a tube-style curve, then high-passes the result at 90 Hz so what comes back is the harmonics, not more sub.

    SB-03
    Tone Bank
    EQ-12 Parametric · 12 Band
    AB
    Explain

    Twelve bands — a 40 Hz low shelf, ten peaking bands from 80 Hz to 8 kHz, and a 14 kHz high shelf — sharing one Q. Drag a knob, double-click resets, scroll trims; Q shapes every band. Pull a band node on the screen and the matching knob turns; Flat returns every band to 0 dB.

    Every knob in the rack takes the keyboard: Tab to it or click it, then ↑↓ to turn it — Shift for fine, PgUp/PgDn for coarse, Home/End for the ends, 0 to reset. Arrow keys land on the same values the mouse can reach, so you can dial an exact setting instead of hunting for it.

    SB-04
    Dynamics Control
    DYN-1 · Program compressor
    Key
    AB
    Gain Red.0.0 dB
    Explain

    Program compressor on the whole mix. Threshold, Ratio, Attack and Release drive the browser's own dynamics stage — a Web Audio DynamicsCompressorNode with its knee fixed at 6 dB; Makeup is plain gain after it. The Gain Red. ladder is the compressor working: it reads the stage's real reduction value, so it moves while audio plays and rests at zero otherwise, and the same number swings the VU needle on SB-05. A ladder parked at zero means no reduction, not a broken meter — the VU face says COMP OFF or COMP AT 1:1 when that is why.

    SB-06
    Master Bus
    Center · mid/side
    Center (mid/side)
    Explain

    The classic mid/side trick on any stereo mix — center-panned vocals solo or vanish. Physics, not stem separation; mono files have no sides to keep. Normal leaves the image alone; Solo Center keeps only what both channels share; Remove Center keeps only what differs between them. Bypass plays the untouched file through the same output — hold it to compare for as long as you hold, click to latch; Loop starts the track again when it reaches the end.

    Explain

    Two passes: the loudest sustained 15-second window, then the loudest 30 — each then snapped to the nearest downbeat.

    Scans your track's real energy for the loudest sustained 15- and 30-second stretches — level detection, not taste; trust your ears on the final pick. The window is then snapped to the nearest downbeat using the same beat grid SB-13 measures, because a clip that starts mid-beat reads as a mistake however good the audio is. It only snaps when the grid is confident and the move is under half a bar — a hook dragged most of a bar to reach a downbeat is no longer the hook it found — and the panel says which of those happened. Preview plays that window, Snippet WAV bounces it through the whole rack, and Vertical video records a 720×1280 WebM with a live spectrum ring (keep this tab visible while it records; phone editors convert WebM to MP4).

    Reads anything your browser can decode — MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG. Decoding is your browser's, so anything it can play it can convert; the container and the sample maths are ours. Resampling is your browser's own resampler; the headers and the quantising are written here, and dropping to 16-bit applies TPDF dither so the error stays uncorrelated noise instead of quiet grit.

    Loudness is measured on the actual output, after resampling, then a single clean gain change — no limiting, no compression. If the target would push the true peak past that platform's ceiling, the gain is capped so it lands exactly on the ceiling instead, and the status line says by how much it fell short. Turning a master down is lossless; only turning one up can clip.

    Explain

    BPM comes from a spectral-flux onset envelope autocorrelated over 60–190 BPM; key from a 12-class chroma correlated against the Krumhansl-Schmuckler major and minor profiles. Both are estimates with a score attached, not readings off a tag — autocorrelation genuinely cannot tell a tempo from its half or double, so both are offered rather than guessed at.

    Tempo: pitch stays put — WSOLA time-stretch. Key: length stays put — resample plus stretch. Honest time-domain DSP that holds up at the ratios people actually use (0.5× to 2×), and smears transients if you push it far. Runs in this tab.

    Source

    Against published targets

    Explain

    Peak metering tells you about clipping and nothing about how loud you'll actually sound — this is the number every platform normalises by. Measured to the standard, not approximated: K-weighting derived for your file's sample rate rather than the 48 kHz coefficients printed in the spec, 400 ms blocks with both required gates (absolute at −70 LUFS, relative at −10 LU) so silence and quiet passages can't drag the average down, loudness range per EBU 3342, and a true peak found by 4× oversampling because the peak between samples is what converters and lossy encoders actually see. That method under-reads by up to about 0.7 dB in the worst case, which is why mastering ceilings sit at −1 dBTP rather than 0. Verified against the published EBU Tech 3341 compliance cases.

    The targets are the figures the platforms publish, not something this app checks with them, and they do move. Every service turns a loud master down — that costs nothing. Turning a quiet one up is where the true peak comes up with it, which is the only case that can clip.
